01 / RESEARCH CONTEXT

Overview

Dichromatic patterns superimpose the reference lattices of two grains in a common coordinate system. They offer a geometric way to examine lattice correspondence, coincidence sites, and possible shared periodicity. In projected views, retaining axial layer information distinguishes apparent two-dimensional overlaps from sites coincident within the same axial phase. This matters when interpreting how the two grain lattices relate, particularly away from exact coincidence-site-lattice orientations.

DichromaticMap provides layer-resolved visualization and quantitative geometry for simple cubic (SC), face-centered cubic (FCC), and body-centered cubic (BCC) grain boundaries. It identifies exact same-layer coincidences, finds nearby lattice-site pairs without moving sites, and searches for common cells under prescribed homogeneous-strain limits. The viewer also measures crystallographic vectors and counts sites in selected cells. Its NumPy-based numerical core is available through the Python library and powers the browser application on the user's device.

02 / ANALYSIS

Analysis capabilities

Exact coincidence, local near-pair matching, and strain-assisted common-cell search are separate geometric operations.

01

Axial layers

Display the distinct axial phases in projected SC, FCC, and BCC reference lattices.

02

Exact coincidences

Identify same-layer coincident sites and inspect exact CSL cells when available.

03

Local near pairs

Find nearby lattice-site pairs within a chosen distance while preserving their positions.

04

Strain-assisted cells

Search for shared periodic cells under a prescribed homogeneous-strain limit.

05

Vectors and counts

Measure crystallographic directions and count lattice sites in selected cells.

03 / APPLICABILITY

Scope and limitations

DichromaticMap analyzes reference-lattice geometry for cubic tilt grain boundaries. Local matching preserves the lattice-site positions, whereas strain-assisted cell searches apply homogeneous geometric deformations. These operations do not perform atomic relaxation or minimize elastic energy.
